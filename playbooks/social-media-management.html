01

Choose the goal, then the channel

A channel earns investment by reaching a defined audience and moving them toward one measurable next step.

  1. Establish the objective: choose discoverability, qualified demand, customer response or retention. Name the audience, the action, the baseline date, and the result that would change your decision.
  2. Shortlist channels: score audience evidence, format fit, action path, operating hours and risk, each 0–2. A zero on audience evidence, coverage or access is a hold regardless of total. Use first-party conversations, referral patterns or buyer interviews, not follower-count guesses.
  3. Choose a mode: invest in one primary channel, maintain service coverage where customers already ask for help, and time-box one secondary test only if staffing allows.
  4. Write the test: capture owner, date range, minimum exposure or sample, time cap, primary KPI, comparison, and the continue/iterate/stop thresholds before launch.
Example · Local catering

Start with Instagram if customers already ask about events there. Test qualified catering inquiries for four weeks with a six-hour weekly cap. If the minimum exposure is reached and qualified leads still miss the agreed floor, pause growth activity; keep checking existing customer DMs.

Start / stop gate

No launch unless the account has a primary and backup, a safe action path, a measurable goal and a staffed reply window. If exposure is too low, label results inconclusive; run at most one bounded extension if the learning is worth the hours. Pause immediately if safety, coverage or access fails.

02

Run capacity across brands

Protect service, client commitments and incident cover before adding another campaign or channel.

  1. On Monday, list billable/team hours after meetings and absences. Reserve incident overflow, then reserve each brand’s inbox coverage, committed reviews and reporting. Confirm backup coverage before allocating experiments.
  2. Rank what remains: P0 safety, access and active incidents; P1 customer commitments and fixed launches; P2 proven repeatable work; P3 growth tests. Ties go to the tighter customer deadline, then the cost of waiting.
  3. Give every active item a brand, owner, due time, hours and decision. Keep queues and permissions brand-specific, even if the same operator runs them. Assign one backup per brand and one handoff window per day.
  4. If work exceeds available hours, move, shrink or cancel P3 first. The Social Lead logs what changed and who was informed; only an authorized owner can change a client commitment.
Example · 30 available hours

Brand A coverage 9 h, Brand B coverage 7 h, fixed work 7 h, incident buffer 4 h, growth test 3 h. A five-hour incident takes the four-hour reserve plus one hour from the growth test; neither inbox is dropped.

Accept more work only if

Coverage is still staffed and the lead can point to available hours or an explicit tradeoff. Use the production system for work scheduling; keep this capacity decision visible in the operating record.

04

Grow organically and work with creators

Start with a question real customers ask, then earn distribution through useful ideas, active community participation and credible partners.

Organic growth: build a repeatable loop

  1. Listen: collect recurring public questions, buyer objections and partner conversations. Pick one you can answer better with a demonstration, a decision aid or original proof.
  2. Choose the action: decide whether the intended reader should save, share, visit a page or inquire. Fix the destination and profile first so attention can convert.
  3. Distribute: answer relevant comments, participate helpfully where the audience is already active, and invite partners to share only when the material serves their audience. Do not buy engagement, spam messages or manufacture praise.
  4. Experiment: compare a repeatable series with its baseline; change one major variable at a time. Hold the measurement window and paid support constant when possible; separate paid reach from organic when reporting.
  5. Reinvest: use qualified discovery, saves per reach, relevant profile visits or qualified actions as appropriate for that goal. Retire formats that consistently fail after the predefined sample and budget.

Creator and influencer collaboration: from shortlist to renewal

  1. Shortlist by fit: score audience match, geographic relevance, quality of dialogue, ability to demonstrate the offer, reliability and brand safety. Review sample posts and recent audience evidence where available. Investigate abrupt growth or irrelevant comments rather than treating them as proof of fraud.
  2. Design a bounded pilot: select one outcome, one creator or small cohort, one trackable path, a spend cap and a review date. Record creator fee, product, usage costs and team time separately.
  3. Confirm terms before outreach commits anything: scope, deliverables, timing, compensation, approvals, honest claims, disclosure, content rights and release, usage channels/duration, paid reuse permission, exclusivity if needed, and correction/takedown process. The brand approver signs off on spend and terms.
  4. Check pre-launch: verify the destination, tracking link/code, permissions and disclosures on the actual format. For U.S. campaigns, disclose material connections such as payment or free products clearly with the endorsement. Use the FTC’s influencer disclosure guidance; cover applicable local requirements before launch.
  5. Audit live and review: capture the final link, verify disclosure and claims live, and track qualified actions and complete costs. Remove audience duplicates where possible; report tracking limits rather than crediting every sale to the creator.
Example · Creator pilot

Choose a local events creator for a catering demo. Compare qualified inquiries from the creator’s tagged booking link with the agreed pilot floor, then decide whether to renew. Keep event size, service area and date eligibility consistent with ordinary lead qualification.

Go / no-go

No activation without audience fit, an owner, approved terms, a verified destination, disclosure and permission for the planned usage. Renew only when the agreed outcome and brand-safety checks support it.

05

Pause, respond, resume

The first decision is whether scheduled activity could make the situation worse; the second is who can speak for the brand.

  1. 01 · OperatorPause affected activity
  2. 02 · Incident LeadConfirm scope & facts
  3. 03 · Client approverApprove response
  4. 04 · Lead + ownerAuthorize restart
  1. Pause: for an account takeover, leaked data, major safety allegation, severe misinformation or insensitive scheduled post, halt affected posts, boosts and auto-replies using the authorized owner. Keep unaffected brands separate. If control is uncertain, notify the account owner and security contact immediately.
  2. Respond: open an incident record. Capture the time, affected accounts, verified facts, unknowns and evidence links. Name one incident lead and one spokesperson, and start an update schedule. Route privacy, security and legal questions to the designated owner rather than improvising.
  3. Use a holding statement only when approved: “We’re aware of the reports and are checking what happened. We’ll share verified updates here.” Do not name an individual, assign blame, promise remedies or publish a timeline without verified approval.
  4. Resume: the lead verifies containment and confirms that a primary and backup can monitor; the client approver clears revised content, timing and any public follow-up. Restart in stages and recheck the next shift. Log the cause, what worked and one prevention owner and due date.
Restart gate

No restart without verified control of the account, verified scope, a cleaned queue, named coverage and documented sign-off.

Example · More reach, same demand

Reach rose 40% in a matched four-week window, but both periods produced six qualified inquiries. Keep the cadence constant and test whether the booking link converts engaged visitors instead of commissioning more posts. This is an illustration, not measured JML results.

Record the decision

Brand/channel · objective · date range · result versus baseline · limitations · continue / iterate / pause / stop · owner · next review date.

07

Define roles and protect account ownership

The client retains primary ownership and recovery. JML operators get named access only for the tasks they own.

R does the work, A makes the final decision, C is consulted, I is informed. In small teams one person can cover several operating roles; client approval or account control still stays with the designated client owner.

08

Onboard and offboard without losing control

Brand onboarding

  1. Confirm the client owner, backup approver, disputes route, working hours and active accounts. Capture the offer, audience, restrictions, verified support routes, brand voice, moderation and objectives.
  2. Audit the registry and baseline; validate named seats, permissions and recovery custodian; test the inquiry route and escalation with a sample case. Require brand-owner sign-off before live operations.

Team member onboarding

  1. Assign a scope and backup; provision minimal access only after client owner approval. Walk the operator through the inbox, moderation limits, private-data handling and incident pause path.
  2. Have the operator route a mock complaint, show the next owner and checkpoint, and demonstrate their access boundaries. Sign off before solo coverage.

Team member or brand offboarding

  1. Before the handoff, assign a successor and inventory open cases, scheduled activity, assets, creator obligations, reporting and integration owners. Use the client-approved destination for exports and transfer only authorized material.
  2. At the agreed cutoff, confirm the client’s replacement access, remove former roles and obsolete integrations, and rotate any legitimately shared secrets via the owner. Remove access promptly for unplanned departures; don’t disable client-owned services blindly.
  3. Verify the recipient can open the handoff, confirm old access is gone, and record ownership, open items, retention/disposition and final sign-off.
Close gate

The successor can operate the accounts, open cases have accepted owners, the client holds recovery, and no departing agency or team access remains beyond explicit written authorization.
