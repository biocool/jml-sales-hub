GTM Strategy Playbook

How we build a client's go-to-market blueprint from evidence, then stay beside them through the first weeks of launch.

The route

Eight phases. Research comes before any recommendation, the client approves the plan before we build the launch kit, and we review the first 30 days together.

When each phase happens

What a GTM strategy answers

What makes ours different

Evidence, labeled

Every number says where it came from: measured, client-supplied, benchmark or our estimate. Gaps are listed, not papered over.

Built to be used

A document the team opens every week, with a calendar, a dashboard and scripts. Not a deck for a drive.

We stay for launch

Weekly check-ins through the first 30 days, so the plan survives contact with real customers.

Build the strategy

Weeks 3 to 5Six decisions, made in order, each following from the evidence

3.1 Pick the launch customer

SegmentBetaLaunch, month 1Growth, months 2–6Scale, months 6–12
A Launch customer
B Second segment
C Partner or referrer
D Later market
Main focusLight testEach persona card: who, why they buy, value per year, cost-to-win target, where to reach them, when we switch them on.

3.2 Position against what they use now

Claims we can make

  • What the client does, stated plainly ("a fixed price, guaranteed in the app")
  • Backed by a number or a source
  • Aimed at the top complaint about the alternatives

Claims we avoid

  • "The only…" or "the best…" without proof
  • Claims about what competitors fail to do

3.3 Price it and work out what's left

Left per sale = price − costs of that sale
Break-even sales = fixed costs ÷ left per sale
Customer value = yearly spend × margin ÷ churn
Customer value ÷ cost to win ≥ 3

The last line is the common rule that a customer should be worth at least three times what it cost to win them. For one-time buyers, use the first sale only.

3.4 Choose channels that pay

BranchCan a bought channel clear the line?
YesPaid ads can lead, with a small first test to confirm real costs.
NoLead with earned reach: partners, referrals, direct outreach, search. Use a small paid test only to measure.

First estimate for paid search: take the click cost Keyword Planner shows for the client's city and divide it by a realistic conversion rate. That is the cost per lead to compare with the line.

Earned channels take longer to start, so begin the slow ones (search, referrals) at launch even if they won't pay off until later.

3.5 Sequence the launch

Before anythingLicences, permits and insurance in place
Beta · ~2 weeksBrand, tools, first list, tracking
Launch · month 1First paying customers, fastest revenue first
Growth · months 2–6Repeat customers, break-even
Scale · months 6–12Second segment, more capacity

Each stage ends with written exit criteria (for example "3 paid bookings and revenue above $2,000 a month"), not a date alone.

BranchWhat kind of business is launching?
Service businessSell first where the client can deliver well today; protect quality before volume.
MarketplaceBuild supply first, in one small area, before spending on customers. A thin supply makes both sides quit.
ProductMake sure stock, delivery and returns work before the launch push.

3.6 Set the measures and the risks

3 to 5 governing numbers

Cost to win a customer→Can we afford growth?
Lead-to-sale rate→Is the message landing?
Revenue vs break-even→Does the business survive?
Repeat or referral rate→Will it compound?

Each one tied to a way the business fails. Set number targets only on measured costs; until then, say so.

What we never do

Applies to every GTM engagement
✕Present a guess as a factEvery number is labeled.
✕Set targets on assumed costsMeasure first, or say targets come later.
✕Promise revenueWe commit to the plan, not the outcome.
✕Target everyoneOne launch customer first.
✕Recommend a channel that can't payNot even "to get going".
✕Write claims we can't defendNo "the only" without proof.
✕Reuse another client's strategyEvery decision traces to this client's evidence.
✕Send the draft coldAlways walk through it live.
✕Disappear after deliveryThe day-30 review always happens.
