Email & Outreach Playbook

How we run cold outreach that books meetings, and owned email (newsletters and automations) that turns an audience into customers, without burning a domain along the way.

The route

Two tracks share one foundation. The outreach track writes to businesses that haven't heard of the client; the owned track writes to people who asked to hear from them.

When each phase happens

Two kinds of email

Outreach (cold)Owned (opt-in)
Who gets itDecision-makers at businesses that fit the client's ideal customerPeople who signed up, bought, or asked for something
Sent fromSeparate look-alike domains, never the main oneThe client's main domain
Looks likePlain text, short, from a personDesigned newsletter or plain, from the brand
ToolsSales Navigator, Apollo, ClayMailchimp or beehiiv, ManyChat
SuccessPositive replies and booked meetingsClicks, purchases, list growth
Biggest riskBurned domains and spam complaintsA list that stops caring

Cold email is for business buyers only. Consumers are reached through owned email and social capture.

3

Messages

Weeks 2 to 4Short cold sequences people answer; owned emails people look forward to

Outreach: a typical sequence

Cold email rules

  • Plain text, under 80 words for email 1
  • Open with something true about them
  • One proof point, one easy ask ("worth a look?")
  • Subject: 2–4 lowercase words, looks internal
  • Each follow-up adds something new
  • Opt-out line and postal address

Never in a cold email

  • Images, HTML templates, attachments
  • A link in email 1
  • Open or click tracking
  • Fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subject lines
  • "Just checking in"
  • A 30-minute call request in the first touch

Spam complaints

When results are poor: find the leak

A/B tests that teach something

  • Change one thing at a time
  • Write down what you expect first
  • Wait for enough sends: 200+ per version for cold
  • Judge cold tests on positive replies, owned on clicks

Tests that waste a month

  • Changing subject and body together
  • Judging cold email on opens
  • Calling a winner after 30 sends
  • Adding volume to fix a low reply rate

Protect deliverability

When something breaksStop the damage first, investigate second
TriggerDo this now
Bounces over 2% on a campaignPause it; re-verify the list
Inbox placement under ~70%Pull that mailbox or domain; back to warm-up
Spam complaints near 0.1%Pause; cut volume; review targeting and copy
Domain on a major blocklistStop sending from it; swap in a spare
Cold reply rate under ~1% after 300+ sendsStop the campaign; fix targeting or offer, not volume
LinkedIn or Meta warning on an accountStop all outreach from it for a week; cut volume

Every incident: tell the client the same day what happened and what we're doing.

What we never do

Applies to every email and outreach account
✕Cold email from the main domainLook-alike domains only.
✕Cold email consumersBusiness buyers only.
✕Add people to a newsletter without sign-upNo bought or scraped lists in owned email.
✕Send an unverified listBounces burn domains.
✕Keep emailing after a "stop"Removed everywhere, same day.
✕Scale volume to fix low repliesFix the targeting or the offer.
✕Fake "Re:" or urgencyHonest subject lines only.
✕Automate a profile we can't afford to loseLinkedIn steps by hand.
✕Spam comments or DMs with ManyChatRotate wording, disclose automation.
✕Promise reply or open ratesWe commit to the process.
