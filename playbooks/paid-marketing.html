Paid Marketing Playbook

The steps we follow for every paid ads client, from the first audit (while they are still a lead) to scale or offboarding. Each phase opens with a diagram and its key rules. Use the switch below to show only Google or only Meta.

What Paid Marketing includes

Media buying

  • Free pre-sale audit with a budget recommendation
  • Strategy, budget split and targets
  • Tracking setup on Google, Meta and GA4, including sending CRM results back to the platforms
  • Campaign build and launch
  • Weekly in-house monitoring and optimization
  • Ad copy for every ad we run
  • Free: one campaign landing page per campaign, as copy and a suggested wireframe
  • A monthly report

Ad creative

  • Static image ads we design ourselves (a large share of what we make)
  • CGI and motion graphics made from scratch
  • Video edits of the client's own footage
  • Captions, end cards, and every size each placement needs
  • 4 creatives per campaign per month

Not included

  • Filming or live-action shoots
  • Photography (statics use the client's photos, our CGI renders, or licensed stock)
  • UGC creators, influencers, actors
  • Custom music composition
  • Building the landing page, unless the client uses JML web services
  • Project files (3D scenes, design and edit files). Clients get the finished ads, uploaded to their ad accounts.

If a concept needs any of these, the client supplies it or we quote it as a separate project.

0.4

Size the opportunity Shared

Google Use Keyword Planner to estimate monthly searches and typical cost per click for their main services in their area. Meta Check the rough audience size for their area and customer type in Ads Manager. These numbers keep the budget recommendation grounded in real demand.

MBDemand and cost estimate
0.5

Work out the numbers that decide the budget Shared

Ask for three numbers: what an average customer is worth, the profit margin, and what share of leads become customers. Multiply them to get the most they can pay for one lead and still break even.

Example: $2,000 job × 40% margin × 1 in 4 leads closes = $200 per lead to break even. Our target cost per lead sits comfortably below that number.

AL · MBUnit economics sheet
0.6

Pick one primary conversion Shared

Choose the single action that counts as a win: a form sent, a phone call longer than 60 seconds, a booked appointment, or a purchase. Other actions (page views, button clicks) can be tracked, but the platforms are never told to aim for them, or they will find cheap clicks instead of customers.

Review their website Shared

Every campaign gets its own landing page copy and wireframe from us (see 4L), so this check is about what we're working with: can we add tracking, is the site fast on a phone, and which existing pages could carry traffic if a campaign page is late. Note what a dedicated campaign page would fix. That makes the free landing page a concrete part of the pitch.

Check how leads are handled Shared

Leads go cold quickly: one called back within minutes is far more likely to buy than one called the next day. If they're slow to respond, the ads will look like they're failing when the real problem is follow-up. Note it and raise it kindly. Our AI and automation team can set up instant text replies and CRM alerts.

Ad platforms learn from the conversions we send them. If tracking is wrong, they learn the wrong thing and spend the budget chasing it. That's why tracking is set up first. A few pieces depend on the build (call assets and the campaign landing pages), so they are finished in Phase 4, and the test is repeated on the final pages before launch (6.1).

Lock the account settings

4G.3

Research keywords and group them by intent

Use Keyword Planner, past search terms, and the words the client's customers actually use.

  • High intent: service + location, "near me", "emergency", "cost", "quote"
  • Mid intent: "best", comparisons, reviews
  • Skip: DIY, "how to", "jobs", "free", "salary", "course"

Start with phrase and exact match. Broad match lets Google go beyond the keyword list, so add it only once smart bidding has about 30 conversions a month to learn from.

4G.4

Build the negative keyword list

A shared list applied to the whole account from day one: jobs, careers, salary, free, DIY, training, course, used, and anything the client doesn't offer. It is the cheapest way to stop wasted spend.

4G.5

Structure the campaigns

Keep brand searches (people typing the client's name) in their own campaign. They are cheap and convert well, and mixing them in makes everything else look better than it is. Split other campaigns by service or area when budgets or goals differ. Keep ad groups tightly themed, roughly 10 to 20 related keywords each.

4G.6

Write the search ads

Responsive search ads: up to 15 headlines (30 characters each) and 4 descriptions (90 characters each). Cover the keyword, the location, the offer, proof, and a call to action. Pin a headline in place only when legally required. Aim for an Ad Strength of Good or Excellent.

4G.11

Keep AI Max off

AI Max is a newer Search setting that lets Google match searches beyond our keywords, write its own headlines, and choose the landing page. We don't use it for now. Google switched many Search campaigns over automatically in September 2026, so open each Search campaign's settings and confirm all three parts are off.

4G.12

Local Services Ads (now inside Performance Max)

The client pays per valid lead (call, message or booking), not per click, and the ads show only on Search and Maps. Eligibility needs licenses, insurance and background checks, plus a complete Google Business Profile with reviews. 4M.4

Keep the structure simple

Meta merged its manual and Advantage+ setup into one flow, and Leads and Sales campaigns now start with Advantage+ settings switched on. Meta learns per ad set. Fewer ad sets means each one gets enough conversions to learn. Start with one prospecting campaign using campaign-level budget (Advantage campaign budget) and 1 to 2 ad sets. Add a retargeting campaign once the website audience is big enough, roughly 1,000 visitors in 30 days.

4M.5

Set audiences

  • Location: a radius around the service area, set to people living there for local services
  • Minimum age only if there is a real reason
  • Advantage+ audience is the default. Interests, customer lists and lookalikes count only as suggestions, and Meta can go beyond them. Location and minimum age are the only hard limits.
  • Excluding people by interest is no longer possible. To keep existing customers out, upload them as a custom audience and add it as an exclusion.

4M.6

Placements

Ads now run across all placements. Meta removed ad-set-level placement exclusions in August 2026 (a few controls remain at account level), and Threads became a default placement in October 2026 with no full opt-out. So every ad needs 9:16 and 4:5 versions, or it gets cropped badly. Preview each placement, including Threads, before saving.

Build the ads

  • Launch with the month's 4 creatives, each a different concept, not tiny variations. New creatives are added each month and the weakest are paused.

  • Headline of about 40 characters, plus a call-to-action button
  • Add several text options so Meta can mix them

4M.8

Set the budget against the learning phase

Meta's guide is about 50 conversions per ad set per week to finish learning. At our $1,000 minimum, ad sets usually still finish learning, just more slowly. Keep to one or two ad sets so conversions aren't split, and don't treat a slow learning phase as a reason to change things.

4M.9

Set the attribution window

Since March 2026, Meta only counts a "click" when someone actually clicks a link. Likes, shares, comments and video views of 5 seconds or more now fall under a separate 1-day "engage-through" window, which is on by default. For lead generation, use 7-day click and 1-day view (Meta's standard), and turn engage-through off (or report it in its own column) so reported leads aren't inflated. Don't compare cost per lead from before and after March 2026 directly.
