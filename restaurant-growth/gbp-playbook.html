01 · First audit

Find what prevents a guest from acting

First priority: fix the wrong branch, incorrect open/closed status, broken menu or checkout, and misrouted calls before adding photos or posting more often. A profile can be highly visible and still send guests to the wrong place.
P0 · Guest blocked

Repair immediately

Wrong pin or hours, disconnected phone, closed-provider checkout, menu routed to another branch, reservation portal naming the wrong venue, or lost ownership.

P1–P2 · Friction & growth

Test after the basics

Missing service options, unreadable menu, outdated prices or photos, slow review response, category mismatch, unclear attribution. Fix the gap with the clearest guest impact first.

15-minute walk-through: On mobile as a guest, find the exact branch in both Google Search and Maps; compare with the restaurant’s own page, entrance/signage, current menu and POS/provider. Tap Menu, Directions, Call, Order and Reserve where applicable. Check the actual landing branch, offered service and first meaningful step. Do not place a real order or reservation without approval. Log device, surface, time, observed result, screenshot with no guest data, owner, next check and status.

Companion: JML restaurant field-sales checklist. Use a single documented issue in a sales conversation, not an invented revenue estimate.

02 · Ownership & setup

Build a reliable profile for each eligible location

  • Access. Get written authorization. Find and request access to an existing profile rather than creating a duplicate. Keep the restaurant as primary owner and invite named JML operators as Managers where sufficient; no shared credentials. Record the exact Maps URL, profile/location identifiers, owner, backup, provider contacts and approval scope. Explain in writing that GBP itself is free and management is a separate service. [G1] [G2] [G3]
  • Identity. Use the real-world name shown on signage. Use the most specific accurate primary category available; add only necessary secondaries that describe the business, not every dish or amenity. Verify customer-facing address, entrance pin, branch phone and branch URL. Each eligible location has its own record. Never add keywords to a name or move a pin for visibility. [G1] [G4]
  • Hours. For a dine-in restaurant, use hours when guests can sit and eat; if there is no dine-in, consult Google’s restaurant fallback for takeout, drive-through or delivery. Enter holiday/special hours and eligible service hours separately. Keep overnight shifts, split services, last seating and kitchen closing accurate on site and provider pages. Verify after a holiday or temporary closure. [G1] [G5]
  • Attributes and media. Confirm pickup, accessibility, outdoor seating and other eligible attributes with the GM before marking them. Use actual, recent photos of entrance, seating, popular dishes and pickup signage; avoid synthetic depictions of the food or storefront. No guarantee Google will display your chosen cover. [G6]
Ghost kitchen or shared address? Do not clone listings. Google applies distinct conditions for delivery-only brands and co-located pickup brands. Check eligibility, permanent signage for customer-facing brands, and distinct packaging/site where required. Hide a delivery-only shared-kitchen address and use the applicable service area. Check documentation before creating or editing a profile. [G1]
Holiday-hours response: Confirm each branch’s date and hours, even when unchanged, and submit them ahead of time. If accepted, verify public Search and Maps; if pending near the holiday, use an owner-approved website and phone fallback while monitoring; if not approved, check the accuracy and next support step. Google says edits can sometimes take up to 30 days. Special hours · Edit statuses

[G1]Call action targets the right venue; directions stop at the guest entrance, not the loading dock. Staff know what to do with takeout and accessibility questions.

Direct vs. marketplace is a business decision. Compare branch fulfillment, cancellations, order mix and contribution per fulfilled order. Include commissions, packaging, discounts, payment costs and incremental labor. Direct links can help preserve margin, but the best route depends on customer acquisition, capacity and service quality. Compare matched service windows and keep partner and direct orders distinct. [E4]

Example, not a benchmark: A dinner-only venue with reservation availability should prioritize Reserve. A counter-service cafe should prioritize pickup and accurate hours. A walk-in neighborhood bar may prioritize directions and menu. Tag only links your measurement stack can ingest; never trade a working guest path for a tracking tag.

04 · Reviews & discovery

Earn trust without manipulating ratings

A neutral review workflow

Invite all eligible recent guests the same way, for genuine feedback. Use a Google review link or QR on a receipt or follow-up. No freebies, discounts, review gating, staff quota, required phrases, or asking guests to post on the premises under pressure. Never compensate a guest for changing or deleting a review. [G11] [G12]

Turn feedback into operations

Review the inbox on workdays. Acknowledge the specific issue, avoid personal or order details in public, and offer a private follow-up. Route safety/allergy complaints to the GM immediately. Report reviews only for a policy violation. Track recurring wrong hours, cold food or slow pickup and assign a fix to the responsible team.

Receipt copy: “Thanks for visiting. If you’d like to share how it went, you can leave an honest review here.”

What Google confirms: relevance, distance and prominence are primary considerations; Google mentions reviews and positive ratings as potential contributors. There is no published formula, guaranteed position or payment to improve organic local rank. Measure relevant queries from multiple fixed locations near demand, not only one search at the door. [G4] [E6]

What JML treats as an experiment: posts for events, actual photos, and refreshed location content can answer real guest questions. Set a practical cadence based on changes, not a claim that daily posting, geotagging, or a fixed review count moves rankings. Check current post policies before publishing, especially for alcohol-related promotions. [G13]

05 · Scorecard

Report the journey without inventing attribution

MetricWhat it isWhat it is not
Profile views & search termsGBP visibility, using Google’s own deduplication and reporting windows. Search terms are monthly; reports can include activity from organic and Google Ads. [G14]All impressions, all Google users or purely organic traffic.
Menu, website, calls, directionsMenu interactions, site-link clicks, call-button taps and directions requests as defined by Google. [G14]Unique people across actions, answered calls, visits or revenue.
Bookings and ordersRead GBP’s applicable metric definition; eligible provider-completed bookings are distinct from link clicks. Cross-check reservation provider and POS; track cancellations and fulfillment. [G10] [G14]Walk-in guests, net reservations, seated covers or every order driven by GBP.
Business outcomeProvider/POS counts of fulfilled orders, seated covers and contribution, with observed source only when reliably captured.Total sales caused by GBP. Label unmatched outcomes or “source unknown.”

Tracking recipe: Where the receiving site accepts and preserves parameters, tag the GBP website/menu link with utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp&utm_content=menu_branch01; use distinct content values and keep casing stable. Check the landing and checkout journey on mobile. Do not append tags to vendor links that break or strip them; record “unattributable” instead. If record IDs match between provider and POS, deduplicate; do not double count or join data on guessed identity. Store only aggregate or approved operational data. [G15]

Illustrative 28-day report: 1,050 to 1,120 profile views; 136 to 168 menu interactions; 31 to 40 tracked, fulfilled direct orders. Menu interactions per 100 profile views went from 13.0 to 15.0. That's an activity ratio, not a person-level conversion rate. The 9 additional orders are observed in a matched period, not proof the profile changes caused them.

Report one comparison with a verdict: Show raw numerator and denominator; use weekday-matched windows, separate locations and service modes, flag ads, weather, events, capacity, holidays and closures. If feasible, stagger a noncritical change across similar stores with pre-period trend checks. Don't delay urgent corrections for a test. At low volume, extend the window and report uncertainty before claiming lift.

07 · SOPs & handoff

Run this with a small team

Roles: Owner/GM approves material changes and maintains the source of truth; JML operator edits, checks and logs; shift lead flags errors and guest feedback; analyst reconciles results. One person can hold several roles. Sensitive edits get a second checker where feasible; changes still need owner consent. [G2] [G3]

  • Daily: triage review and profile alerts, urgent closure, failed provider or phone. Consult the merchant before reversing Google-suggested changes.
  • Weekly: mobile-check guest paths, take care of new menu/hours/photos, verify fix status against screenshots and log unresolved items.
  • Monthly: share a matched-window scorecard, log outliers, review ownership and holiday schedule, and recommend one next experiment.
  • Urgent incident: preserve public evidence and exact location, notify the owner, restore the last working approved path, and verify both Search and Maps. For suspension, collect the necessary matching business evidence before opening Google’s appeals flow; no duplicates or repeat submissions while pending. [G16]
  • Offboarding: document current branch settings, live links, unresolved cases and reports; confirm owner access and remove agency access. Google requires the client to be able to disassociate and regain exclusive control within seven business days of notice. Remove a departing team member’s access without removing the profile; transfer open issues and verify access for the successor. [G3]

08 · Evidence & sources

What the evidence actually supports

Google defines its policies and metrics. Independent work informs priorities, not promises. Commercial surveys and case reports are labeled, not treated as randomized tests.

Source & designVerified findingHow to use it
Luca, Nagaraj & Subramani, working paper · Published version (2026)
Natural experiment and temporal variation.		The published study estimated a 5–10% revenue increase after adding a first listing on the studied review platform. The working-paper methods identify Yelp and Texas alcohol-serving businesses.Supports prioritizing an accurate presence if missing. Not a Google experiment, continuing optimization result or sales promise.
Michael Luca, HBS working paper
Historical Washington State restaurant revenue and displayed Yelp rating cutoffs.		Estimated 5–9% higher revenue per displayed Yelp star, driven by independents; no effect detected for chains in that sample.Invest in real service quality and honest feedback; do not borrow that percentage for GBP forecasts.
Anderson & Magruder, Economic Journal (2012)
Regression discontinuity in historical Yelp ratings.		At the threshold, an extra displayed half-star increased measured dinner-slot sellouts by 19 percentage points.Shows demand can respond to visible reputation signals. This is not 19% more bookings, Google conversions or seated covers.
Li & Wang, Management Science (online 2024)
Restaurant sales and delivery platform participation.		Marketplace delivery substituted for some independents’ own takeout and complemented chains’ takeout in the study.Track direct and marketplace outcomes separately. Do not infer that a Google ordering link caused the results or that extra sales equal profit.
Toast diner survey (2026)
Vendor survey, 1,466 US adults who had recently used a restaurant; October 2025 fieldwork.		18% cited map apps as a discovery channel; 16% cited search engines.Keep Maps and the branch site consistent, but the survey is self-reported and neither percentage is a share of GBP-attributable sales.
ReplyOnTheFly restaurant scans (2026)
Vendor sample: 57 restaurants and 1,996 geographic scan points.		Median rating was 4.7 for top-three results vs. 4.5 at ranks 11–20; those groups had comparable median review counts.Use multiple geographic points and record keywords. Repeated appearances, owner-selected keywords and uneven geographic coverage limit inference; not evidence to buy ratings or ignore review volume.
BrightLocal consumer review survey (2026)
Vendor-sponsored survey, 1,002 US adults.		Age of reviews and how businesses respond were reported as factors in consumer decisions.Make it easy to leave recent, genuine feedback and respond specifically. General local businesses, self-reported behavior; not a restaurant rank experiment.
Vizion / Buca di Beppo case report (2025 comparison)Agency reported 309% more booking clicks during a program that bundled profile, site and migration work.Use the distinction between booking clicks and confirmed reservations. The case does not isolate any one change or provide a transferable lift.
