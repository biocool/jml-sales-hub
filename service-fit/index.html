FOR JML · IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

For the JML team.

Place the assessment after the service benefits and before the package or consultation CTA. Keep the primary CTA available for visitors who prefer to skip it.

How the scoring works

For service checks, answers indicating a larger gap earn 0–3 points. Equal-weight scoring converts answered, applicable questions to 0–100. Readiness questions are shown separately and never inflate need. Package checks measure suitability, not a problem.

0–33: lower priority or lower fit. 34–66: worth exploring. 67–100: stronger signal. These are editorial rules, not research-derived thresholds. A number requires at least 60% of scored questions, rounded up. Unknown answers are excluded; incomplete results remain provisional. Even a complete questionnaire with unknowns is labeled limited evidence.

How do the interactive controls work?

A four-stop slider adds tactile interaction to ordered answers, without inventing extra numerical precision. Every slider also has labeled tap targets, arrow-key support and an explicit “Not sure” choice. Other questions use large choice cards or simple lists. The result gauge is read-only: it visualizes answers rather than letting visitors adjust their score. Workflow cards use simple diagrams to explain the choices. A brief fade marks each question change; reduced-motion preferences disable the effect.

Coverage & deployment notes

All 14 service-detail URLs in the live JML sitemap checked November 9, 2026 are represented here, each with exactly five questions. The service index can link to this selector. This is a public review prototype, not an update to jaymarketinglab.com.

Website implementation can reuse these HTML/CSS/JS assets or port the data to the existing component framework. Use a service-specific deep link such as #web-design. Review and approve each assessment before integrating it into a service page. No forms, analytics or CRM submission are connected.
