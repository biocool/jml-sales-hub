Capture the right information at the right time

Visit minimum (before leaving)
  • Business name + location or public URL
  • Visit date/time and salesperson
  • Spoke to: name + role, or explicitly “not provided”
  • Decision maker: known / unknown / not present
  • One business contact method if voluntarily provided; do not require both email and phone
  • Permission for follow-up: yes/no/unknown + channel, purpose, date and source
  • Observed need or concise neutral notes
  • Outcome + next action + due date + owner
Before qualification
  • Contact role and decision authority
  • Service family: GBP, social, web, SEO, automation, or other
  • Location count and entities/brands involved
  • Current provider / process, when volunteered
  • Need, scope, urgency, desired outcomes
  • Budget status: confirmed range / discussed but unknown / not discussed
  • Buying process and other approvers
  • Access/assets feasibility and risk flags
Before proposal
  • Opportunity ID linked to account and contacts
  • Scope, quantities, exclusions and approval responsibilities
  • Setup, monthly recurring, one-off project and pass-through costs separately
  • Discount type, basis, approver and proposal version
  • Minimum term, payment schedule and cancellation terms
  • Expected close date with reason; not a promise
  • Next meeting or follow-up agreement
Before handoff
  • Signed agreement and approved scope
  • Payment status, not card or bank credentials
  • Billing contact and start date
  • Profile/site ownership and approved access method
  • Delivery owner accepts kickoff checklist
  • Baseline measurement and agreed reporting cadence

Clean up the past. Build a better habit.

Separate one-time scope

Review catch-up

Inventory unanswered historical reviews before quoting. Agree the date window, response count and prioritization. Start with recent reviews; do not blindly answer every years-old review.

  • Separate from the 30 new monthly replies.
  • Personalized drafts, owner-approved voice and review log.
  • Health, safety, refunds, legal allegations and private details require owner escalation.

Illustrative estimate: for ordinary replies. Adjustable inputs; not an agreed rate. Complex cases quoted separately.

Separate campaign

Review request setup

Build a neutral request flow, official Google review link and QR card. SMS/email activation is optional and requires permission, channel compliance and a verified list.

  • Having a phone number is not consent to automated messages.
  • Ask all eligible guests for honest feedback; no review gating, rewards or discounts for reviews.
  • Respect opt-outs, identify the restaurant, suppress duplicates and keep consent evidence.
  • Provider, number, carrier and registration charges separate. Confirm actual rates before launch.

Setup: quote after workflow, channels and integration audit. QR assets in tier 2 do not include SMS sending.

How to read “human” versus “automated”

  • Explicit automation: Merchynt Paige and Peace Call disclose AI use.
  • Explicit human involvement: Web Market Florida says a person reviews content; GBP Expertise names a dedicated specialist; Spark N Code describes an owner-operated studio. None of these claims proves an entirely manual workflow.
  • Not disclosed: Geografixx, Local ØRBIT and CyberTek’s reviewed offer pages do not quantify AI use or provider-side human review. Ask before treating them as human-only benchmarks.

These are provider statements, not an audit of their actual operations. Client approval, human QA and human-written content are three different things.

Restaurant field sales playbook

Practice guide, not permission to contact anyone. Ask permission, diagnose before pitching, and keep tentative prices separate from an approved quote.

Interactive conversation map

Permission → Decision maker → Fit → Locations → Scope → Objections → Quote → Approved next step

Capture only what helps qualify

  • Restaurant name, location count and branch decision authority.
  • Who owns Google access; current agency or internal manager.
  • New reviews/month versus historical unanswered backlog.
  • Incorrect hours, menus, links; recent posting/photo activity.
  • Owner-supplied photo/footage availability and approval contact.
  • Priority outcome, budget range, timing and agreed next step.

No passwords. Request Manager access only through an authorized invitation. Do not claim an audit finding unless you checked it.

Tier guidance, not automatic upselling

Essentials: upkeep and consistent responses; 4 GBP posts + 30 new review replies/month.

Local Growth: Essentials plus QR/review process and competitor checks. No included bulk SMS.

Google + Social: Growth plus 8 core content pieces adapted for Instagram and Facebook, not 16 unique creatives. Confirm usable source material.

Over 30 new reviews, filming, community management, many branches or unusual approvals → scope review before quoting.

Sample conversations

1 · A respectful walk-in

Salesperson: “Hi, is this a bad time? I’m with Jay Marketing Lab. We help restaurants keep their Google profile accurate and respond consistently to reviews. Who looks after that here?” Staff: “The owner is busy.” Salesperson: “No problem. Is there a better time, or may I leave a short summary? I won’t interrupt service.” Outcome: Do not pitch the staff member as if they can approve a purchase.

2 · Diagnose before recommending

Owner: “What would you actually do?” Salesperson: “First, who updates your hours and menu links, and roughly how many new reviews arrive each month? Are there older unanswered reviews?” Owner: “Around 20 new reviews and 80 old ones.” Salesperson: “The base plan covers up to 30 new replies monthly and 4 Google posts. The 80 older reviews would be a separately scoped catch-up job, not silently included.” Outcome: Confirm profile condition and classify backlog separately.

3 · Multiple branches

Owner: “We have three locations.” Salesperson: “Do you approve marketing for all three, and does each have its own Google profile? Are photos, menus and approvals centralized?” Owner: “Yes, but one branch gets 70 reviews a month.” Salesperson: “Then we should scope that branch separately. Prices are per location; I can request a multi-location proposal, but I can’t promise a discount or unlimited replies.” Outcome: Escalate review volume and multi-location terms.

4 · Price and free tools

Owner: “Google is free. Why pay a monthly fee?” Salesperson: “The Google profile is free. You would pay for the recurring work: keeping details current, preparing four posts, handling up to 30 new replies and reporting what was done. The current planning price appears in the Settings-driven quote below and remains subject to a written scope. If you already do this consistently, you may not need us.” Owner: “Can you guarantee top three?” Salesperson: “No. We can commit to the agreed work and reporting, not a Google ranking.”

5 · Existing agency or negative reviews

Owner: “We already have an agency.” Salesperson: “Then let’s avoid duplicate work. Do they handle Google posts, review replies and profile updates? If there’s no gap, we shouldn’t add another service.” Owner: “Can you delete bad reviews?” Salesperson: “We can flag potentially policy-violating reviews for assessment, but Google decides removal. Genuine criticism is handled with a professional response; sensitive complaints go to you before posting.”

6 · Quote, consent and follow-up

Owner: “Let’s do it. Can you waive setup?” Salesperson: “I can request that, but it isn’t approved. We’ll send a written scope covering location count, setup, monthly fee, minimum term and approval process before anything starts.” Owner: “Can you text all my old customers for reviews?” Salesperson: “Only after we verify appropriate consent and opt-out handling. That campaign is separate; we won’t send selectively to happy customers.” Salesperson: “Would you prefer a short proposal or a scheduled review of the profile? What contact method do you authorize for that?” Outcome: Record the agreed purpose and channel; no automatic follow-up or enrollment.

Escalation rules

  • Any setup waiver, negotiated discount or changed term → Javad approval.
  • Negative delivery margin or missing cost data → stop and rescope.
  • Privacy, legal accusations or sensitive review → owner approval before reply.
  • SMS consent unclear, purchased list, review gating or incentives → do not launch.
  • Ownership dispute or suspended profile → specialist assessment, no restoration promise.
  • Unclear branch authority → verify decision maker before quote or access.

Safe next-step checklist

Summarize verified needs → recommend scope → disclose per-location price and exclusions → request approval for concessions → deliver written proposal → obtain agreement and access → schedule onboarding.

Record an authorized follow-up channel and date. “Not interested” means stop. Do not promise rankings, review removal, revenue, launch dates or unapproved services.

Commercial assumptions

Economics model

Direct cost = hours × loaded hourly cost + tool allocation. Contribution margin = (service price − direct cost) ÷ service price. This is not net profit; sales, tax, overhead and payment fees may remain.
